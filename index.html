Information about the Hantavirus

Key Points

  • The Andes virus is part of the Hantaviridae family and is a rodent-borne virus.
  • The virus can be transmitted through an infected host and contaminated materials.
  • The Andes virus' main carrier is the long-tailed pygmy rice rat.
  • Symptoms initially appear flu-like but can progress to respiratory and cardiovascular complications.

Symptoms

The incubation period for the Andes virus is typically between one and six weeks.

After the initial symptoms begin, the illness can quickly progress to a severe cardiopulmonary phase.

Transmission

Transmission of the Andes virus can occur through contact with an infected host, including contact with contaminated urine, saliva or droppings.

Andes virus can also be transmitted between people in certain circumstances.

Andes Virus (Orthohantavirus andesense)

The Andes virus is a species of hantavirus belonging to the Hantaviridae family. It is a single-stranded, negative-sense RNA virus belonging to the genus Orthohantavirus.

Its primary host is the long-tailed pygmy rice rat (Oligoryzomys longicaudatus).

The virus primarily affects the lungs, blood vessels and cardiovascular system.

Hantaviridae Family

The Hantaviridae family contains viruses associated with rodents, shrews, moles and bats.

Relative Viruses

The Hantaan Virus

The Hantaan virus is a relative of the Andes virus and is associated with haemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS).

Proposing vaccination solution for the Hantavirus

Key Points

  • The proposed vaccination will be an inactivated vaccine using an inactivated version of the Andes virus.
  • The vaccination will include adjuvants to enhance the immune response.
  • Stabilizers will be included to help maintain stability during transportation.
  • Unit-dose packaging will be used.

Reliability on the Andes vaccine: The Hantavax vaccine supporting feasibility

The proposed Andes virus vaccine is informed by Hantavax, a vaccine developed to protect against the Hantaan virus, a close relative of the Andes virus. Hantavax was designed to stimulate the immune system to recognise and respond to Hantaan virus. While the proposed Andes virus vaccine is not intended to replicate Hantavax, it adopts several vaccine components and design principles that have been used in vaccine development.

This concept of using existing vaccine blueprints as the basis for developing new vaccines is not new.

Other vaccine components in the Andes vaccine
Residuals

Residuals are tiny amounts of various substances used during vaccine manufacturing that are not active ingredients in the completed vaccine.

Diluent

A diluent is a liquid used to dilute a vaccine to the correct concentration immediately prior to use. One commonly used diluent is sterile water.

Preservatives

Preservatives can prevent contamination in some vaccine formulations. Some vaccines do not contain preservatives because they are supplied in single-dose vials.

The importance of a vaccine

Vaccines are important tools for developing immunity or protection against infectious diseases. Project Viral's creation of a hypothetical vaccine for the Andes virus demonstrates how vaccine development can be considered when addressing serious infectious diseases.

Prevention of the Hantavirus

Key Points

  • Avoid being around rodents and areas where rodents live.
  • Avoid close contact with people who are unwell if possible.
  • Follow good hygiene practices.
Although the Hantavirus does not have an official widely available vaccine, prevention methods can reduce exposure.
Rodent Safety

People should avoid contact with rodent urine, droppings, saliva and nesting materials. Rodent control and exclusion from homes are important strategies for reducing exposure.

Caring for pets safely

Safely caring for pets, especially pet rodents, can help reduce potential exposure to hantaviruses. Pet owners can consult veterinarians about appropriate health precautions when introducing new animals.

Cleaning up after rodents

If you have mice or rats in or around your home or vehicle, it is important to clean up urine, droppings, dead rodents and nesting materials safely.

FAQ

Key Points

  • There is currently no specific widely available treatment that directly eliminates Andes virus.
  • Patients may require hospitalisation and supportive care.
  • Testing is generally considered when symptoms and relevant exposure history suggest infection.
  • Public health authorities monitor cases and contacts to reduce further transmission.
Is there a treatment for the Andes virus?

Currently, there is no specific antiviral treatment that reliably eliminates Andes virus. Supportive therapy, including assistance with breathing and management of complications, can be important for patients.

Will the Hantavirus become a pandemic?

A global pandemic caused by Andes virus is considered unlikely because transmission is generally associated with specific exposure circumstances rather than widespread everyday respiratory transmission.

Can you test for the Hantavirus?

Diagnosis can involve laboratory testing of blood or other clinical samples to determine whether a person has been infected.

How many people are infected with Hantavirus and where are they?

Case numbers can change as investigations continue. Public health authorities publish updated information about confirmed and suspected cases.
